# Polynomial presentation of a unitriangular stage

The actual stage coordinate ring over any commutative ring `K` is the quotient
of the full unitriangular coordinate ring on `Fin n` by the ideal generated by
strict-upper matrix entries `(i,j)` with `j.val < i.val + r`. The indices left
after quotienting are the strict-upper pairs with `i.val + r ≤ j.val`.

Import `AlgebraicGroups.Algebra.UnitriangularStagePolynomial`. The public type
`UnitriangularStageCoordinateRing.SurvivingPair n r` records these indices.
The `K`-algebra equivalence
`UnitriangularStageCoordinateRing.polynomialEquiv K n r` has type
`CoordinateRing K n r ≃ₐ[K] MvPolynomial (SurvivingPair n r) K`.
It uses the **actual** stage quotient, not a different quotient presentation.
No nontriviality, field, domain, positivity or finiteness hypothesis on `K`,
`n` or `r` is needed beyond `[CommRing K]` and `n r : ℕ`.

`polynomialEquiv_surviving` sends the class of a surviving universal entry to
its polynomial variable, and `polynomialEquiv_forbidden` sends forbidden entries
to zero. `polynomialEquiv_entry` combines both cases as a dependent conditional;
`polynomialEquiv_symm_X` gives the inverse image of each variable. The two
`polynomialEquiv_C` declarations preserve coefficients in either direction.
The separately exposed `survivingInclusion`, `killForbidden`, `toPolynomial`,
`toPolynomialQuotient`, and `fromPolynomial` support direct algebra-map use.

The proof uses the published `UnitriangularCoordinateRing.freeEquiv` to identify
full strict-upper coordinates with a multivariable polynomial ring. The pinned
mathlib `MvPolynomial.killCompl` projection kills exactly forbidden variables,
while `rename` includes surviving variables. The projection annihilates the
*accepted* stage ideal, so `Ideal.Quotient.liftₐ` gives a map from the actual
quotient. In the reverse direction, the quotient applied after `freeEquiv` and
`rename` maps generators to their universal entries. The composites agree on
all polynomial generators, and `Ideal.Quotient.algHom_ext` descends the second
identity. No cancellation argument is used, so the construction also applies
to `ZMod 1`. At `n = 0` or `1`, and whenever `r ≥ n`, there are no surviving
variables; the result is an algebra equivalence to the empty-variable
polynomial algebra, not a definitional equality with `K`.

This is a commutative-*algebra* presentation, not a Hopf-algebra or additive-group
comparison. The native Hopf stage and underlying affine scheme are supplied by
the separate [stage guide](../../GroupScheme/UnitriangularStages/README.md) and
[affine-space guide](../../GroupScheme/UnitriangularStageAffineSpace/README.md).
No group-homomorphic section, represented quotient, descent, smoothness,
flatness, base-change tower or source-coverage statement follows from this
algebra equivalence.
